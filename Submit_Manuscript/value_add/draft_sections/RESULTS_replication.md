## 3.z Cross-cohort replication separates RDH5 from the remaining anchors

Anchors were tested for replication beyond the UK Biobank discovery sample in two
independent settings. In the well-powered **continuous refractive-error** data
(Tedja et al. 2018 / CREAM), four of the five anchors replicated with concordant
direction: RDH5 (β = −0.0935, *P* = 3.3 × 10⁻³³), CD55 (β = +0.0286, *P* = 0.008),
CTNNB1 (β = +0.0493, *P* = 6.9 × 10⁻⁸), and FBN1 (β = −0.0440, *P* = 5.8 × 10⁻⁴);
only **TGFB1** was discordant, reversing direction between discovery and replication
(β = −0.159, *P* = 0.005). In **FinnGen**, using the clinically-defined high-myopia
endpoint (H7_MYOPIA; 8,266 cases / 254,189 controls), **only RDH5 replicated**
(OR = 1.22, 95% CI 1.04–1.43, *P* = 0.013); CD55 (OR = 0.98, *P* = 0.54), CTNNB1
(OR = 0.96, *P* = 0.68), FBN1 (OR = 0.93, *P* = 0.54), and TGFB1 (OR = 1.35,
*P* = 0.62) did not.

Two points follow. First, that RDH5, CD55, CTNNB1, and FBN1 replicate in continuous
refractive error is consistent with their status as **established myopia loci**
(Section 3.x, novelty audit): **replication alone does not distinguish them.** The
discriminating filter is **colocalization** — only RDH5's gene-expression signal
shares a causal variant with myopia — so for CD55, CTNNB1, and FBN1 the locus
replicates but the specific gene's expression cannot be assigned as the causal driver.
Second, the FinnGen high-myopia nulls are consistent with this ordering but should be
read cautiously: FinnGen captures a more severe phenotype with fewer cases and wider
confidence intervals (notably for the single-instrument anchors), so its nulls reflect
phenotype and power differences in addition to the colocalization pattern. RDH5
replicating even in this smaller, clinically-ascertained endpoint further marks it as
the one anchor with cross-cohort, cross-phenotype causal support. (RDH5 direction is
concordant across all cohorts without sign transformation.)
